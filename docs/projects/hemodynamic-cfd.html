Hemodynamic CFD Study of a Pulmonary Artery

FAU

Personal project, supported by FAU | 06/2026 | Independent study

From MRI-derived model to flexible print

AI / SimulationCFDAutodesk CFDSLAFlex 82A

What?

  • Personal interest: how does blood flow through a branched vessel tree, and can a simulated geometry also become a physical model?
  • Geometry: MRI-derived human pulmonary artery model, after Tang et al., Annals of Biomedical Engineering (2011).

How?

  • Meshed and simulated the vessel tree in Autodesk CFD with a prescribed inlet velocity of 10 cm/s.
  • Evaluated pressure, velocity, wall shear stress and vorticity.
  • Printed the vessel in flexible resin (Flex 82A) by SLA, with printing support from FAU.

Results

  • Pressure falls by about 550 dyne/cm² from the main artery to the distal branches.
  • Velocity rises to about 30 cm/s in narrow branches; wall shear stress peaks at the bifurcations (about 20 dyne/cm²).
  • Simulation results were not compared with measurements.
